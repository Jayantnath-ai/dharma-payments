You do not tune friction.
You tune risk appetite.

Friction is a consequence, not a setting. The only dial is how expensive you consider a human review, and every position on it buys less interruption with more exposure. Below is the actual measured curve, on 3,000 real-shape invoices after shadow calibration.

Rows in grey are dominated: past $500 you accept more loss and get essentially nothing back, because human reviews have already fallen to noise. That is the end of the efficient frontier, and it is where the honest recommendation stops.

The metric that matters is the human interrupt rate, not total holds. An automated test payment costs about a dollar and the finance team never sees it; a human review costs analyst time and goodwill. Reporting both as one friction number, which the earlier version of this demo did, understates the gate badly. Exposure is expected unrecovered fraud over the 90 day window, in which six attempts were made.
