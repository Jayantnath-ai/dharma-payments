The finding: shadow calibration is not a refinement, it is what makes the gate deployable. Untuned, 62% of payments reach a human and no finance team keeps that switched on. Calibrated and tuned, 9% do, while protection holds. The incumbent process is comfortable and catches one fraud in six. Note that holds are not one thing: an automated test payment costs about a dollar and nobody sees it, a human review costs analyst time. Only the second is real friction.

Earned trust over time · friction falls, protection holds

Each month the protocol recalibrates on everything observed so far, including a 10% exploration channel that releases low-value escalations anyway so labels never fully censor. Friction falls 60.7% → 14.7%. Fraud caught across the period: 7 of 8. This is the answer to "your gate escalates too much", only at first.

Recurring domestic vendors earned near-total discretion across ~1,200 observations. New international payees earned almost none across 34. Adhikāra as scope granted in proportion to demonstrated judgment, per segment, rather than one global limit.

Adversarial stress tests · rejected design vs shipped design

The first design bound trust to segment labels, "recurring vendor", "domestic major". Those are attributes an attacker selects. Mimic the label, inherit the trust. The shipped design binds trust to payee identity, account fingerprint, and the amount envelope that earned it, with uplift capped by irreversibility.

The first two attacks are fully closed, the shipped design matches the static baseline exactly, because a changed fingerprint or an out-of-envelope amount voids earned trust before it can be spent. The third is only partially closed, and that is the honest limit: if an attacker presents the real payee, the real account, and an ordinary amount, nothing in the transaction distinguishes it. That case needs out-of-band verification, not better inference.

What the fix actually cost

The rejected design's numbers were not real. They were borrowed against a vulnerability, trust extended to forgeable labels. Measured properly, with identity-bound trust and the risk dial at its recommended setting, 9.2% of payments reach a human. The rejected design's apparent advantage disappears once it is attacked. Protection in normal operation is identical across all three (3.6 of 4 fraud caught, same exposure).

Shadow mode is the only period with uncensored labels: once a gate blocks, blocked transactions never resolve and the training distribution is censored exactly where the gate refuses. Fraud is far too rare to calibrate on (8 events in 180 days), so trust is learned from the abundant signals instead, confidence reliability across thousands of ordinary payments, human override direction, and realized recovery outcomes. Second executable component of DharmaAGI, after the Viveka Gate.
